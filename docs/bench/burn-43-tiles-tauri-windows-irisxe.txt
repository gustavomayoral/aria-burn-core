aria-burn-core burn-43 at 384ee96 (main 9d6d3cf, BURN-38's vertex windows, plus the ?auto=tilesweep bench; no sim

"\GPU Process Memory(*)\Shared Usage" for the browser GPU process (mem2.ps1). The laptop's Claude app and WSL stayed
open; free RAM before each run is given. No build, test or other bench ran.

?auto=tilesweep: the 5 µm 100 mm raster (19.7 M samples, birch with the thermal step) at this GPU's default job cell
budget (50 M → 15 µm, 6671 × 6680 cells). Per GPU budget: a new GlSim({ gpuBudgetBytes }), fit (thermal step, timed
but not part of full-res), one cold full run, 5 warm full runs, then an untimed run that reads every tile core back
and hashes the stitched dose and verdict.

Summary
- The premise of BURN-43 is gone: since BURN-38 the per-sample data sits in bounded vertex windows, fit leaves
  341 MiB resident, and the 768 MiB default now plans 15 µm as 2 × 2 tiles of ≤ 3347², not 7 × 7 of ~953²
  (BURN-37). Warm full-res at 768 MiB, 15 runs over 3 fresh processes: median 1286 ms, max 1653 ms (BURN-37 at 7 × 7:
  median 1583, max 1830). Cold 862 / 1464 / 1308 ms.
- Only two plans exist for this job: 2 × 2 (pool 182 MiB) up to ~1063 MiB, and one tile of 6680² (pool 723 MiB)
  from ~1064 MiB. 1024 MiB plans the same 2 × 2 as 768; 1280 and 1536 the same 1 × 1.
- One tile is faster warm: median 1072 ms, max 1413 ms (15 runs, 3 fresh processes), ~200 ms under 2 × 2. But:
  · The WebView2 GPU process peaks at 2018 / 2583 / 2064 MiB (fresh processes) and up to 2793 MiB in the sweeps,
    against 1068–1070 MiB at 768. That is at or over the ~2.37 GB level where WebView2 lost the context in
    BURN-38; there is no margin.
  · Free RAM falls to 182–305 MiB (19 MiB once in a sweep) from ~6.8 GB, so the laptop pages. At 768 it stays
    above 1.6 GB.
  · Cold isn't better: 1175 / 1631 / 1999 ms vs 862 / 1464 / 1308 at 768.
  · Edge lost the context once in 3 fresh runs at 1536 MiB (burn-43-tiles-edge-windows-irisxe.txt).
  · Not bit-identical: one tile deposits unbinned (time order), several tiles deposit through the spatial bins
    (bucket order), so float blending sums in a different order. Dose hashes 20715b68 (2 × 2) vs 5e91c9bf (1 × 1),
    verdict ff77a0e5 in both. Within multi-tile plans the hash doesn't change with the tile side (768 vs 1024 here;
    10 × 10 vs 2 × 2 on the M1). The bench's "FAIL results bit-identical" lines are this 1 × 1 vs 2 × 2 difference.
- No context loss in WebView2 in any run (8 processes, 26 budget steps).
- Decision: keep the Intel-integrated default at 768 MiB. No gain worth the memory: the default already plans the
  largest multi-tile split, and it is under 2 s with ~350 ms to spare.
- In-process sweeps are noisier than fresh processes: after a 1 × 1 step has starved RAM, the next steps run slow
  (2 × 2 warm 1154–2262 ms after 1 × 1 in run 2, vs 758–834 ms when 2 × 2 ran first in run 1).
