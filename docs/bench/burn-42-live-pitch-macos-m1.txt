BURN-42 live-job pitch in the real Aria Burn app — macOS 14.6.1, WKWebView ("Apple GPU"), Apple M1,
on AC power, run 2026-11-04. NOT Windows/Iris Xe. Job cell budget 256 M (JOB_CELL_BUDGET_LARGE).

What was measured: the job grid's pitch (SimSetup.grid.pitchMm) at every live refresh, while a
G-code file streams into the app's GRBL TCP emulator, against the same file opened from disk.
- Job: the 100 mm bench raster (BURN-35's job) as G-code: 100 × 100 mm at (10, 10), bidirectional,
  0.1 mm line interval, 1001 lines, G1 F6000 M4 S300, 2007 lines in all. Provisional 10 W diode,
  σ 0.03 × 0.015 mm → 5 µm detail pitch, 410 × 400 mm travel; birch (thermal).
- Sender: a Node script streaming like LightBurn/LaserGRBL's buffered mode (character counting
  against GRBL's 128-byte RX buffer, one line per ok), emulator pacing "real time", so the stream
  takes as long as the job (~1093 s).
- App: aria-burn `pnpm tauri dev` (debug Rust, vite dev server) linked to aria-burn-core main.
  Two local, uncommitted bench patches: setGrid POSTs every grid to the dev server, and
  VITE_EMULATOR_PORT=2323 overrides the saved emulator port (23 can't be bound from the sandbox).
- Clean run: the stream started after 30 s with no vitest / cargo / rustc / tsc process, and a
  watcher saw none during it. An earlier run of the same stream overlapped another session's
  cargo test / vitest for its first 42 s and was discarded; its pitch timeline was identical
  (5 µm until 702.9 s / 12,633,910 samples, then 10 µm, 10 µm at job.loaded).

  same file opened from disk       10 µm (k = 2)      10,020 × 10,020      -

- Before: k came from the travel area (410 × 400 mm at 5 µm ≈ 6.6 G cells against 256 M), so the
  stream ran at 30 µm from the first refresh (the before run was stopped after ~1 min of the
  stream; every refresh logged 30 µm, 3334 cells across the 100 mm width).
- After: k comes from the bounds so far. The raster has its full 100 mm width from the first line
  and grows downward; it stays at the 5 µm detail pitch until the 5 µm grid would pass 256 M cells
  (20,000 × 12,760 = 255 M at y = 73.6 mm), then steps once to 10 µm and stays there. The stream
  ends on exactly the file's pitch and grid, so job.loaded changes nothing.
- The live refresh itself runs on the coarse scrub grid (≤ 1024 cells a side, ~98 µm here), so its
  cost doesn't depend on k; the step rebuilds the job grid and coarse checkpoints the same way bounds
  growth does on every refresh. The live thermal plan doesn't depend on k (aria-burn-core
  thermal-live.test.ts checks a stream with k steps stays bit-for-bit equal to a full run).
