BURN-35 live-job bench (incremental thermal step) in the real Aria Burn webview — macOS 14.6.1,
WKWebView ("Apple GPU"), Apple M1, on AC power, run 2026-11-04. NOT Windows/Iris Xe.
Opened with aria-burn's ARIA_BURN_BENCH_URL pointed at this repo's bench (branch burn-35, served
with `npx vite bench --port 5299`) and ?auto=live; the report came back over POST /__bench-report.
The window was a foreground native window (page visible: visible). WKWebView's performance.now()
has 1 ms resolution, so times are whole ms.

Kept only a run with no other test or build process on the machine: another session ran Vitest
in bursts this afternoon, so a script waited for 30 s of quiet, ran the bench, and discarded
runs that saw any vitest / rustc / cargo process (2 of 3 attempts discarded). An earlier run
with ?auto=thermal,live, before the 25 % per-sample headroom for live runs, gave the same
picture (incremental 15–93 ms, fallbacks 250–603 ms on the spike job at 40 chunks).

What one refresh is: the app's live refresh (aria-burn burn.svelte.ts scheduleLive → tick):
new batches arrive, the live setup is rebuilt, the thermal step runs (path "incr": continued
over the new bins, BURN-35; "full": a full run because the live thermal plan changed), the
setup is fitted to the GPU (GlSim.fit, in the "setup" column) and a coarse run at the head.
"before" forces a full thermal run every refresh (the pre-BURN-35 behaviour, same live plan).
"bins" is the number of active thermal bins the step stepped. The job is the 100 mm raster
(1121 s), fed in 10 or 40 equal chunks.

Summary, thermal step per refresh:

                                   before (full rerun)    after, 10 chunks         after, 40 chunks
  spike job, incremental refresh   -                      63–128 ms (3 of 10)      16–81 ms, median 24 ms (29 of 40)
  spike job, full rerun            405–717 ms, grows      304–618 ms (7 of 10)     232–608 ms (11 of 40)
  spike job, last refresh          659 ms → wait 1416 ms  63 ms → wait 218 ms      16 ms → wait 126 ms
  5 µm job, incremental refresh    -                      151–187 ms (3 of 10)     37–163 ms, median 44 ms (29 of 40)
  5 µm job, full rerun             459–1937 ms, grows     455–1677 ms (7 of 10)    280–1512 ms (11 of 40)
  5 µm job, last refresh (thermal) 1937 ms                151 ms                   39 ms

- Refresh cost tracks the chunk, not the job so far: a quarter of the chunk is roughly a quarter
  of the incremental cost (spike 74 → ~17 ms, 5 µm 180 → ~41 ms once the bins are ~1.3 s long),
  and it stays flat from 60 % to 100 % streamed while the full rerun keeps growing.
- Full reruns happen when the live thermal plan changes: here 11 times over the job at 40 chunks
  (the grid's extent crosses a 16 mm block as the raster grows, or binS doubles), at the same
  points of the stream as at 10 chunks. With 10 chunks most refreshes land on one; with the app's
  ~100 ms refreshes they would be about a dozen out of thousands (not measured in the app).
- Incremental vs a full rerun of the same store and plan on the GPU: 0 difference in every
  per-sample eAbs and dose weight and in the bulk map (the GPU-vs-CPU tolerance from the thermal
  bench in this webview is eAbs max rel Δ 2.23e-4, dose weight max |Δ| 1.99e-4).
- Not thermal, and now the bigger part of a 5 µm refresh: GlSim.fit (setup column, 0.1 → 0.36 s,
  it bins every sample spatially once the job spans several tiles; the app's coarse refresh
  doesn't call fit) and the coarse run itself (0.1 → 0.2 s, it deposits every sample so far).
- Comparison with BURN-33's numbers (docs/bench/burn-33-*): those used the job plan, and the
  Chromium ones ran in a hidden pane. The "before" rows here are lower than BURN-33's Chromium
  ones: in WKWebView the full step's CPU statics take 0.2 s for 5.9 M samples, against 1.4 s in
  today's (hidden) Chromium pane, so the gain in Chromium should be larger (not measured).
